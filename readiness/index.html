Investor Readiness

Clarity before commitment.

Before evaluating a private real estate opportunity, make sure the investment structure fits your liquidity, time horizon, risk tolerance, tax picture, and expectations.

This is an educational self-assessment, not an accreditation test, suitability determination, investment recommendation, or public score.

Before a Strategy Call

Answer the questions you would want answered for yourself.

Denali's view is simple: the right next step depends on whether the structure makes sense for you before a specific deal or return projection enters the conversation.

Your information

If you came here from the Blueprint, these fields will be filled automatically. We use them only to connect this readiness submission to your existing Denali contact record.

1. Time horizon

Are you comfortable with a typical private real estate holding period of roughly 5 to 7 years?

2. Liquidity

Would you still have adequate liquidity and emergency reserves outside this investment?

3. Investment objective

What are you primarily trying to accomplish? Select any that apply.

4. Return expectations

Do you understand that private real estate investments do not provide guaranteed income, distributions, or returns?

5. Investor role

Are you comfortable participating as a limited partner rather than the day-to-day operator?

6. Illiquidity

Do you understand that private real estate is illiquid and may not offer an easy way to exit early?

7. Tax context

Have you considered how a private real estate investment may fit your broader tax picture?

8. Risk fit

Does an illiquid private real estate investment fit your current income, risk tolerance, and long-term wealth strategy?

9. Capacity for loss

Could you absorb a partial or total loss of invested capital without jeopardizing essential financial obligations?

There is no numerical score. Your answers simply determine which next step is most useful.

Questions for any sponsor

A disciplined sponsor should be able to explain the downside clearly.

  • What assumptions drive the projected return?
  • What happens if rents grow slower or expenses rise faster than expected?
  • How is the debt structured, and what refinancing risk exists?
  • What reserves are in place?
  • What fees and incentive structures apply?
  • What would cause the investment thesis to fail?
  • What is the exit strategy?
  • How and how often will investors receive updates?
