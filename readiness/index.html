The final readiness question

Before investing, ask yourself:

Do I understand why this investment should work, what could cause it not to work, and whether it fits my personal financial situation?

If the answer is yes, the next step is a focused investor conversation. If the answer is no, slow down and ask more questions.

Questions for any sponsor

A disciplined sponsor should be able to explain the downside clearly.

  • What assumptions drive the projected return?
  • What happens if rents grow slower than expected?
  • What happens if expenses rise faster than expected?
  • What is the debt structure?
  • Is the debt fixed-rate or floating-rate?
  • What reserves are in place?
  • What fees does the sponsor receive?
  • What is the preferred return structure?
  • What is the downside case?
  • What would cause the investment thesis to fail?
  • What is the exit strategy?
  • How often will investors receive updates?

Tax questions to review

Understand the tax picture before committing capital.

Multifamily investments may generate depreciation benefits, but tax outcomes depend on each investor’s situation. Before investing, review:

  • How will K-1 losses be reported?
  • Will paper losses offset qualifying income in my portfolio?
  • Could any losses offset ordinary income based on my tax situation?
  • How does depreciation recapture work at sale?
  • Could a 1031 exchange be available at the partnership level?
  • How will preferred returns and distributions be characterized?
  • Should I invest personally, through an entity, trust, IRA, or other structure?
  • Should my CPA review the offering before I invest?

Denali’s View

Discipline before excitement.

At Denali Equity Investments, private real estate should be evaluated with discipline before excitement. The goal is not to invest because a deal looks attractive on paper. The goal is to understand the assumptions, risks, structure, tax treatment, and operating plan before capital is committed.

A strong passive multifamily investment should be supported by:

  • A real asset with current income.
  • A clear business plan.
  • Conservative underwriting.
  • Thoughtful debt structure.
  • Adequate reserves.
  • Transparent sponsor communication.
  • A realistic exit strategy.
  • Alignment between sponsor and investor outcomes.
